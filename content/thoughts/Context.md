# The literary origins of the Context

In 1999, cyberpunk and transreal poet Rudy Rucker published a book called "Saucer Wisdom" [@ruckerSaucerWisdom1999]. Disguised as a non-fictional report based on the edited notes of an alien abductee named Frank Shook, the book is actually an amazing collection of fantasies about the future of communication, computing, and humanity.

One technology that Frank/Rudy describes is called a Lifebox, a device that holds the recorded and heavily hyperlinked memories of a person. Originally used to create Chatbots that mimic deceased relatives, it soon becomes a kind of business card: the Context. People begin to exchange their Contexts in order to get to know each other even before they meet in person.[^1]

This idea of heavily hyperlinked memories reminds me of Vannevar Bush's Memex.

# Contexts of the Internet of old

When reading about the Context I remembered my first homepage that I spun up as a teenager, back around the time when "Saucer Wisdom" was published. I had no idea for any concept of this website of mine so I made it a reflection of my interests at the time: My reviews of the [Anti-Flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Flag) discography up to this point (which consisted of exactly three albums and two EPs that I knew of) and descriptions of [Flatland BMX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flatland_BMX) tricks.

People used to have way more elaborate websites, be it on Geocities, myspace or whatever and it feels to me that they often served the purpose of Rucker's Context. Finding somebodies personal website -- either by chance or on purpose -- and clicking through their interests was almost a social experience, it was almost like interacting with that person.

The aforementioned Rudy Rucker is an example of somebody who still maintains one of these wonderful oldschool personal websites: https://www.rudyrucker.com. And he also put his [own Lifebox online](https://www.rudyrucker.com/blog/rudys-lifebox/).

When social media came around personal websites mostly disappeared. But the replacement felt differently. And I will have to elaborate at some other time why I think that was the case.

# Digital Gardens

[Digital gardens](https://github.com/MaggieAppleton/digital-gardeners#theory-philosophy-and-navel-gazing) recently became a thing within the PKM community. They can be described as a mix of blog and wiki. A regular writing that is then hyperlinked with the rest of the content. In a way they get closer to the idea of the old personal websites. They also go beyond that concept as they are incomplete. Some ideas are mere sketches, others are outlines or even fully formed essays.

Even though the garden metaphor is a good one, I'd rather call my digital garden my Context. It is the part of my Lifebox that I wish to show to the web wilderness. The rest of it I keep offline: in my Obsidian vault, in handwritten notes and in my head.
